## The Problem

Human presence is finite, asymmetrically distributed, and locked inside formats that don't scale. Three expressions of one structural limit.

### 1. The Access Problem

The most knowledgeable people in any field are structurally inaccessible to the majority who would benefit from their thinking. A coach with 50 active clients can only take so many sessions per week. A CEO with 2,000 employees cannot personally answer every question about the company's vision and strategy. A retired judge has decades of moral reasoning that ends with her. A celebrity with 20M followers and not one of them can have a real back-and-forth with them.

Decades of decision logic exist inside one head. The moment that head steps back, the logic walks out the door.

### 2. The Attention Problem

The knowledge people need already exists. It's in a two-hour podcast, a 300-page book, a sold-out masterclass, a 40-episode YouTube series. But attention spans are shorter than ever and shrinking every year. People can't sit through two hours of content to find the one insight that applies to their situation.

What they actually want is to ask the person who created it: *"What would you do if you were me?"* That's faster, more personal, and more trusted than any content format. But the person who created it is unreachable. Their knowledge is locked in static media that doesn't respond, doesn't personalize, and doesn't know who you are.

Echo unlocks this. It turns static knowledge into a live conversation with the person behind it. And because it can reason by analogy, it doesn't just repeat stored material. It applies the person's thinking to your specific situation.

### 3. The Loneliness Problem

Most people don't have mentors. They want guidance from people they respect but cannot reach them. They don't know who to ask. They don't have anyone in their immediate life who has been through what they're facing. The result: millions of people making major decisions alone, in echo chambers, or asking generic AI that doesn't know who any of them are.

These are the same problem from three angles: human presence does not scale, and the formats we've built to preserve knowledge are passive, impersonal, and increasingly ignored.

**Behind every major problem, a dozen smaller ones compound it.** People are afraid to ask even when access exists, worried they'll waste someone's time. Creators answer the same question for the hundredth time. Online identity is a profile page, not an interactive presence. Networking relies on luck and algorithm, not genuine intellectual compatibility. Monetizing expertise means trading hours for dollars. Lead qualification burns time on people who were never a fit. Context from conversations and meetings evaporates. People want to ask an expert how they'd behave in their situation, and with reasoning by analogy, that's now possible. Everyone wants to scale themselves; nobody wants to be replaced.

## What Echoes Does

An Echo is an interactive digital version of a real person, built from their knowledge, content, experience, and way of thinking. It is a new form of media: personalized, interactive, and grounded in a specific human identity.

For users, an Echo is the fastest way to access someone's thinking. Instead of reading their book, listening to their podcast, or waiting for a reply, you ask their Echo. It answers from their actual material, in their voice, calibrated to your situation. "What would you do if you were me?" becomes a real question with a real answer.

For creators, experts, and founders, an Echo helps them scale themselves: stop repeating the same answers, monetize their knowledge, qualify leads before a real conversation, and learn what their audience actually wants. The Echo handles the first conversation so the real one is better.

This is not a product for one niche. Those who creates content, whether blogs, tweets, books, courses, or podcasts, are already trying to scale their thinking. Echo is the native medium for that. It's horizontal, not vertical.

What changes by use case is who the Echo talks to and what it shares with them. Same Echo for self-reflection, family, public, clients. Audience-aware visibility controls determine what is visible to whom.

### Why Not Just ChatGPT?

"Why not tell ChatGPT to act like Elon Musk and give me business advice?"

Because a generic LLM is a costume, not a person. It can imitate anyone and stands behind no one. There is no consent, no verified material, no accountability for what comes out of its mouth, and no upside for the real person whose voice it is borrowing. The Echo is the opposite on every axis: a specific human's identity, captured with their consent, grounded in their material, governed by them, and economically aligned with them. They earn when their Echo performs. This is not a prompt trick. This is identity infrastructure.

### Onboarding: Your Echo speaks in minutes.

1. **SSO sign-in.** Google, LinkedIn, or Apple. Pulls name, surname, date of birth, country/city. 
2. **Connect everything first.** Connect: WhatsApp, LinkedIn, YouTube, Instagram, TikTok, Substack, Patreon, Notion, Rosebud, Email, Google Drive, podcast feeds. Upload: documents, journals, books, content. All connected sources and uploads are automatically organized in the Knowledge Vault, tagged by source. While the user records their voice intro in the next step, the system is already processing uploads and scraping connected accounts in the background.
3. **1-2 minute voice intro (mandatory, no skip).** "Tell me who you are in 60 seconds, however you want." Creates the Echo's digital voice (active from day one), seeds the RAG corpus, and gives the Adaptive Interview Engine a style baseline against which every future quality score is calibrated.
4. **Echo goes live immediately in "warming up" state.** The Echo is available to the creator instantly after the voice intro, even while content is still processing. The creator can talk to it, test it, and start the self-training loop. Visitors cannot see the Echo yet. As more content processes and the creator answers interview questions, the Echo gains depth. Once the system judges the Echo has crossed the quality threshold, it earns the option to go Public (KYC required).

### Self-Testing: Train Your Echo by Talking to It

Once the Echo is live, the user enters a self-training loop. This should feel like a game, not homework.

- The Echo surfaces 3-4 suggested questions the user can ask themselves (or the user types their own).
- The Echo answers each question as the user would.
- **Deep feedback (optional):** the user can edit the response or give specific notes ("missing context", "shallow", "that's not how I'd phrase it"). This triggers deeper correction in the corpus.
- Periodically, the Echo flips the dynamic: "Let me ask you something to fill a gap." The user answers. This is the Adaptive Interview Engine, integrated into the natural flow. The more the user answers, the smarter the Echo becomes.
- **XP system.** Every interaction earns XP. Voice answers earn a bonus. Hitting XP milestones unlocks public listing eligibility and visibly moves the Fidelity Level, a publicly visible score that reflects how well the Echo represents the person behind it. 

Every correction and every answer enters the corpus as a citable source.

### The Adaptive Interview Engine

The interview is voiced by the user's own Echo asking the user to fill its gaps. Recursive and lowest-overhead UX. Questions are textual; answers can be text or voice.

What it does, every time the user gives an answer:

- Evaluates the response on five quality dimensions: **specificity, reflective depth, narrative presence, emotional authenticity, voice signal** (each 0.0-1.0).
- Produces a composite quality score (0.0-1.0).
- Routes the next question:
  - LOW score: contextual probe ("Can you picture a specific moment when that happened?")
  - MEDIUM score: deepen on the same theme
  - HIGH score: advance to the next gap in the user's coverage
- Tracks coverage across the user's own topics, expertise areas, and life dimensions. The system maps each user's unique territory, not a fixed grid.
- Awards XP based on quality, not length. A 30-word vivid scene scores higher than a 300-word ramble.
- Voice answers earn a +0.10 bonus on voice signal.

The goal: capture not just what someone knows, but how they reason. So when a visitor asks "What would you do in my situation?", the Echo can actually answer.

### Fidelity Level

Every Echo has a publicly visible Fidelity Level. It answers one question: how well does this Echo actually represent the person behind it?

The Fidelity Level is driven by the Adaptive Interview Engine's five-dimension quality scoring. It moves only when the Echo's ability to answer well actually improves, not when raw material is added. This creates a direct incentive loop: the creator's effort makes the Echo better, which makes the score visible, which builds visitor trust, which drives conversions.

The score reflects how complete, authentic, and reliable the Echo is across the topics its person cares about. An Echo with a high Fidelity Level can handle nuanced questions, respond in the person's actual voice and reasoning style, and go deep on their areas of expertise without falling back to generic answers.

**How it moves.** The Fidelity Level increases when the user adds material that makes the Echo more capable: answering interview questions with specificity, uploading content that covers new ground, correcting responses that were off, filling gaps the Echo couldn't answer. It does not increase from volume alone. Dumping 10,000 words of filler, uploading AI-generated text, or repeating material already in the corpus does nothing. The engine measures how much the Echo's ability to answer well has actually improved, not how much raw material was added.

**Why it matters.** Visitors see the Fidelity Level before they start a conversation. A high score signals that this Echo is worth talking to. For creators, higher Fidelity means more visitor trust, which means more engagement, more conversions to their products, and more reason for people to come back. The incentive is aligned: the creator's effort to make the Echo better directly translates to better outcomes.

### Echo-to-Echo Conversations and Interest-Graph Matchmaking

Every Echo on Echoes can talk to every other Echo. Two consequences.

**1. Conversations between users go through Echoes first.**

Instead of cold-DMing someone, you message their Echo. Their Echo answers from their material. If the Echo cannot answer, it queues the question for the real person. You are not interrupting them at 3 AM. You are not wasting their time on a question they have already answered 100 times.

**2. Echoes find each other based on shared interests, not profile filters.**

Because every Echo tracks what topics it has discussed and with whom, the system surfaces meaningful introductions.

Example: *"You spoke with Lewis Hamilton's Echo about cars last week. Mate Rimac is the CEO of Bugatti whose Echo covers similar topics. You might want to talk to him."*

Three signals build the interest graph:

- Conversations the user has had with other Echoes (topics, depth).
- User's stated interests in settings (optional explicit signal).
- Notifications about questions visitors have asked the user's Echo, so the user knows what people are curious about.

Echoes with overlapping interest signatures get surfaced as suggested to talk to. Friendship, professional connection, mentorship, dating; all derive from the same engine. The user picks the lens.

**Privacy default:** opt-in below Fidelity Level threshold (new users have to explicitly enable being suggested), opt-out above (established Echoes are findable by default, but the user can turn it off).

### Privacy and Audience Controls

**Single Echo, multiple visibilities. Per-data-point control.**

Four audience layers determine who sees what: **Guest** (anyone, no account), **Public** (free account), **Inner Circle** (explicit invited list or paid subscription), **Private** (only the creator and collaborators).

Per-data-point tagging: trauma you discussed with a therapist Echo does not appear when your business contact's Echo asks about resilience.

Default: everything captured is Private until the creator surfaces it.

**Knowledge depth is the monetization lever.** Guest and Public visitors get surface-level answers drawn from the creator's publicly available material (podcasts, interviews, articles, social posts). The real depth — proprietary frameworks, knowhow, methods, personal case studies, journal entries, unpublished material — lives behind the paywall, tagged as Inner Circle in the Knowledge Vault. A paid deep session or Inner Circle membership unlocks access to this deeper material for that conversation or on an ongoing basis. This is what makes sessions and memberships worth paying for: the difference between a generic answer anyone could get from the creator's podcast and a deep, personalized response drawn from material that exists nowhere else. Private-tagged material never surfaces to any visitor — it trains the Echo's reasoning but stays invisible.

### Knowledge Vault

The Knowledge Vault is the creator's central knowledge management system. It works like a personal Google Drive for everything the Echo knows: organized, browsable, and permission-controlled.

**What it stores.** Every piece of content the Echo learns from lives in the Knowledge Vault: uploaded documents, journal entries, podcast transcripts, LinkedIn posts, Google Drive files, voice recordings, interview answers, meeting transcripts, blog posts, course materials. Anything the creator connects or uploads is indexed, stored, and browsable.

**How it's organized.** Content is organized into folders the creator manages. They can see at a glance which material came from LinkedIn, which came from their Google Drive, which are journal entries, which are podcast transcripts. The creator can create custom folders (e.g., "Client Frameworks," "Personal Reflections," "Book Draft"), move content between them, and track when material was added and from which source.

**Per-folder and per-item sharing.** Each folder and each item within it has its own visibility setting: Public, Private, or Inner Circle. A creator might make their podcast-derived folder Public, keep their journal entries Private, and share their proprietary coaching frameworks only with Inner Circle members. This is the mechanism that makes audience tiers meaningful — the visibility of knowledge, not just the Echo's availability, changes based on who is asking.

**Source tracking.** Every item in the Knowledge Vault is tagged with its origin: LinkedIn import, Google Drive sync, manual upload, voice journal, interview answer, meeting transcript, Echo Capture recording. The creator always knows where their knowledge came from and can audit, edit, or remove it at any time.

### Digest Summary

Creators on paid tiers get a configurable digest of what their Echo handled (and didn't handle). Starter gets a weekly performance report. Pro and Max get full customization: daily, weekly, monthly, or custom triggers.

Format: "In the past [period], most visitors asked about how you handle nerves before a stage performance. We couldn't find this in your podcasts or materials. Could you tell us?" The creator answers in voice or text. The Echo follows up with two clarifying sub-questions if the engine judges the answer needs more depth. Then it is absorbed into the corpus.

This is the Echo-asks-you-to-fill-its-gaps pattern. Five minutes per session. The creator is not pulled out of their day; they spend that time making their Echo smarter, exactly where it needs to grow.

### Content Creation Tools

The Echo does not just talk to visitors. It creates content for the creator.

Based on what visitors are asking, trending topics in conversations, and gaps the Echo identified, the system suggests and generates content in the creator's voice:

- **Twitter/X posts.** Short-form takes on topics visitors are asking about most. "Your audience asked 47 times this week about pricing strategy. Here's a thread in your voice."
- **LinkedIn posts.** Professional-length content that addresses the questions the creator's Echo could not fully answer, positioning the creator as the authority.
- **Blog posts.** Long-form pieces built from the creator's corpus material, structured around the topics visitors care about most.
- **Social content (Instagram, TikTok captions).** Short, punchy content in the creator's tone for platforms where their audience lives.
- **Newsletter content.** Weekly or monthly roundup of what the creator's Echo discussed, what people cared about, and the creator's perspective.
- **Podcast topic suggestions.** "Your audience asked 120 questions about burnout this month. Here are three episode angles based on what they specifically wanted to know."

The creator reviews, edits, and publishes. Nothing goes out without approval. But the draft is already there, already in their voice, already informed by real audience demand.

This turns the Echo into a content engine. The creator's audience tells the Echo what they want. The Echo tells the creator. The creator publishes. The cycle compounds.

Content creation tools are available on Pro and Max tiers.

### Relationship Memory

Every visitor who interacts with a creator's Echo builds a persistent profile over time. The Echo remembers what they discussed, what they were struggling with, what products they were interested in, and where they left off.

When the visitor returns a week later, the Echo picks up the conversation: "Last time we talked about your pricing problem. How did the client call go?"

This is not just chat history. It is relationship continuity. The Echo tracks progress, follows up on commitments, and deepens the conversation across sessions. For coaches, therapists, and consultants, this is the difference between an AI chatbot and an AI that actually feels like ongoing support.

Portable Echo makes this even stronger: the Echo remembers conversation history AND already knows relevant context the visitor chose to share from their own Echo before the first message.

### Smart Notifications and Live Takeover

Creators can take over a live conversation at any point — the visitor was talking to the Echo, now they are talking to the real person inside the same chat. The Echo handles everything by default, but the system sends smart notifications when someone high-value is in conversation.

**How smart notifications work.** The system identifies visitors who match criteria the creator cares about. The criteria can be explicit (creator sets rules: "notify me if anyone from a Fortune 500 company talks to my Echo") or inferred from context (visitor's own Echo profile, stated profession, conversation depth, engagement signals).

**Examples of smart notifications:**

- A footballer's Echo is being used by a scout from Real Madrid who has been asking detailed questions about playing style for 15 minutes. The footballer gets a notification: "A scout from Real Madrid is in conversation with your Echo right now."
- A psychologist's Echo is talking to a visitor who seems like a strong fit for one-on-one therapy. Notification: "A visitor matching your ideal client profile has been in conversation for 20 minutes about anxiety management."
- A founder's Echo is being queried by a VC partner asking detailed questions about the business model. Notification: "An investor from Sequoia has been talking to your Echo for 30 minutes."

The creator taps in, takes over the conversation live, and the visitor gets a signal that they are now talking to the real person. This is not expected for every conversation. But when it matters, the creator knows, and they act. If the conversation warrants a deeper call, the creator can direct the visitor to book a scheduled live call through the integrated calendar.

Live takeover with smart notifications is available on Pro and Max tiers.

### Provenance

Every Echo response cites its source. If the Echo does not have material to answer confidently, it refuses gracefully and queues the question for the user. No fabrication.

The Echo can reason by analogy across its corpus. If someone asks Taylor Swift's Echo how it feels to play in front of hostile fans at a Champions League final, the Echo does not have that experience. But it knows what it is like to walk onto a stage in front of 100,000 people and how its person handles fear, pressure, and performance anxiety. It draws on that and says so transparently: "I've never played football, but I know exactly what it's like to face a massive crowd when the energy is against you. Here's how I deal with that..."

This is what makes the Echo useful beyond simple Q&A. It can transfer principles across domains, as long as it is transparent about the analogy. This is also what makes it a new medium, not just a retrieval system. A book cannot do this. A podcast cannot do this. An Echo can.

### Echo Capture

A mobile and desktop companion app that serves as the Echo's always-on learning layer. The user carries it with them and it captures material the Echo can learn from.

**What it captures:**

- **Meeting transcripts.** Connects to Zoom, Google Meet, and Microsoft Teams. After a meeting, the app processes the transcript and extracts topics, positions taken, decisions, reasoning, and communication style.
- **Live conversation recordings.** With consent of all parties, the app records in-person conversations (client sessions, mentorship calls, informal discussions) and processes them the same way.
- **Voice journals.** The user speaks their thoughts on the go. Stream-of-consciousness, reflections on the day, reactions to events. The app transcribes and feeds it to the corpus.
- **Written journals.** Quick text notes, observations, ideas. Anything the user wants their Echo to know but would never write a blog post about.

All captured material goes through the user's digest review before entering the active corpus. Nothing is absorbed without review. The user tags each item with visibility (Public, Private, Inner Circle) before it goes live. All captured material is stored in the Knowledge Vault, organized by source type (meeting transcripts, voice journals, conversation recordings, written notes).

Echo Capture turns the Echo from something you build deliberately into something that grows passively from how you actually live and work. The most accurate Echoes will be the ones fed by real conversations, not just published content.

Echo Capture is available on Pro and Max tiers.

### Identity Verification

Two levels. Phone number verification for private Echoes. Full KYC (government-issued ID + social media cross-link + email domain verification) for anyone who wants to go public. The Echo becomes discoverable in search, appears in the public library, and can receive visitors only after KYC is complete.

The Fidelity Level and public verification are independent. A user can have a high Fidelity Level on a Private Echo. Going Public requires both sufficient Fidelity (the Echo must be good enough to represent the person credibly) and verified identity (the person must be who they claim to be).


### Pre-Build (Internal Sales Tool, Not Public)

To remove friction in creator outreach, we pre-build a draft Echo from publicly available content (podcasts, interviews, articles, social, books) before reaching out.

Pre-builds are strictly internal. We never surface them publicly without consent. This is a sales artifact: when we contact a creator, we can say "here is what your Echo would sound like, built from your own public material. Want to claim it?" This is a much stronger pitch than a cold ask.

Pre-build economics: aggressively pre-build the top 500 outreach targets per quarter (high-value creators where claim probability and TAM justify compute cost), and build others on-demand at the moment of outreach.

Pre-builds are text-only. The Echo's digital voice is created at claim, from the voice intro. We do not replicate someone's voice from public audio without their consent.

When the creator claims, they go through standard onboarding (voice intro, uploads, adaptive interview), and the Echo deepens into something only they can produce. Creators who want their Echo publicly discoverable must complete full KYC before going live.

**Consent is existential.** Delphi got C&D letters and lawsuits for unauthorized digital twins. We do not surface an Echo of a real person without consent, full stop.

Subscription revenue is the floor: every paying creator pays a tier fee from day one. The Echo serves as upper funnel (discovery), mid funnel (education, trust), and lower funnel (product recommendation at peak intent). Visitor payments for deep sessions, Inner Circle memberships, and booked calls stack on top. A creator builds their Echo for free. Visitors talk to it for free, within usage limits the creator sets. When a visitor hits a limit or shows deep interest in a topic the creator monetizes, the Echo redirects to the creator's existing offering or signals that deeper access is available through a paid session or Inner Circle membership.

The Echo serves as all three funnels at once. Upper funnel: people discover the creator through their Echo and learn what they are about. Mid funnel: the Echo educates, answers questions, and builds trust. Lower funnel: the Echo recommends the creator's product, session, or event at the moment of highest intent.

Examples:

### Why creators stay

**Private knowledge moat.** Through the guided interview process, creators share insights, reasoning, and personal frameworks that do not exist anywhere else on the internet. This knowledge base, organized and managed through the Knowledge Vault, is exclusive to their Echo.

**Ongoing depth.** Public data (new podcasts, articles, social posts) auto-updates. Audience questions deepen the knowledge base further. Creators test their own Echo and correct it. Collaborators manage the Echo on the creator's behalf. ### "Why would anyone share private data?"

This is the most common VC concern. The answer has four parts.

First, people already do. They tell ChatGPT their deepest secrets and use it as a therapist. They upload legal contracts, medical results, and bank statements to Claude to make sense of them. They journal with AI every morning. The behavior of trusting AI with private information is established. The difference with Echoes is that some of this data becomes public-facing through the Echo. That is why Echoes gives creators granular, per-data-point control over what stays private and what is shared. Every piece of information is private by default until the creator explicitly surfaces it.

Second, the data that makes the Echo valuable is mostly already public: podcasts, books, interviews, social posts. The guided interview adds depth but is entirely voluntary and incremental. The creator controls what is shared, down to the sentence, with per-data-point privacy controls. Nothing enters the knowledge base without review.

Third, all data is encrypted in transit (TLS 1.3) and at rest (AES-256), stored in EU infrastructure only, and isolated per user. Each Echo is a separate LoRA adapter file. If the creator deletes their account, everything is gone. No shared model contamination.

Fourth, the incentive is clear: the more depth the Echo has, the better it performs, the more revenue it generates. Creators share private data because it directly translates to income and audience value.

## Revenue Architecture

Echoes has two sides: creators pay SaaS subscriptions for tools and distribution; visitors pay creators for access and depth.
Creators keep 100% of all revenue their Echo generates for their own products — courses, books, coaching programs, event tickets, anything sold on their own site or through external platforms. The Echo recommends these products at the moment of highest intent, linking directly to the creator's sales page. ### Revenue Streams

**1. Creator SaaS tiers (100% Echoes revenue).** Required for public listing, analytics, monetization tools, and professional distribution. Predictable, recurring, independent of visitor volume.

**2. Platform fee on visitor payments (20%).** When visitors pay for access or depth through the Echoes platform, the payment is split 80/20: creator keeps 80%, Echoes keeps 20%. This applies only to payments that flow through Echoes:

- **Deep sessions.** Visitor pays a one-time fee (€5-€50) for a session of deep-access messages with a specific Echo. Creator sets the session price and the number of messages included (default: 20 messages). Session expires when messages are used or after 24 hours, whichever comes first. The session unlocks the Echo's Inner Circle knowledge base (material the creator has enabled for paid access) for that conversation.
- **Inner Circle memberships.** Visitor pays a monthly subscription (€5-€100) set by the creator. Grants access to one or more invite-only Inner Circle groups with deeper knowledge folders, relationship memory, exclusive content, and expanded message access.
- **Book A Call.** Visitor wants a real conversation with the creator. The Echo surfaces the creator's calendar (Calendly integration or native). Visitor picks a slot, pays through Echoes (€25-€100, creator sets the price), and receives a Google Meet or Zoom link. The actual call happens outside the platform. The Echo provides the creator with a pre-call briefing based on the conversation so far — what the visitor asked, their context, their intent — so the creator walks in prepared.
**3. Brand licensing and keyword bidding (Year 2+).** When many users ask Novak Djokovic's Echo about the best racket, "racket" becomes a trending keyword in his Echo. Head can bid on that keyword. If Novak approves the partnership and genuinely uses the product, his Echo recommends Head racket when the topic comes up. The brand pays Novak (with Echoes' cut) for authentic, consented placement inside trusted conversations. Same logic applies to hotels, supplements, cars, equipment, anything an Echo is frequently asked about. This is not advertising overlay. It is contextual integration driven by real user demand. Same model applies to identity licensing: brands license a creator's Echo voice and perspective for marketing campaigns with the creator's explicit approval.

**4. Multi-Echo conversations (Year 2+).** Per-session only (€15-50, scales with number of Echoes). Unlocks full knowledge access to every Echo in the session. Revenue split 80/20: each creator receives an equal share of 80%, Echoes keeps 20%. Premium product with no equivalent anywhere.

### Why 20% Is Defensible

The 20% applies only to payments that flow through the Echoes platform — sessions, Inner Circle memberships, and live takeovers. Creators keep 100% of all revenue from their own products (courses, books, coaching, events). The 20% covers what makes those in-platform payments possible: per-creator fine-tuned inference, relationship memory across sessions, analytics, conversion tracking, and the full monetization stack.

For comparison: Substack takes 10% and provides no AI, no voice, no analytics, no monetization tools. Patreon takes 5-12%. App stores take 30%. Udemy takes 37%.

Echoes is designed for revenue sustainability from day one. The 20% fee means every creator who generates in-platform visitor payments contributes to platform revenue immediately. The model is built for positive unit economics at small scale, without needing to unlock future revenue layers like brand licensing or keyword bidding before the business works.
For context: Delphi charges 0% on Builder ($79/month) and Scaler ($299/month) tiers, with an Immortal tier at $25,000 anually upfront for celebrities and public figures. Their strategy is to optimize for network adoption first, then monetize through brand licensing, people search, and knowledge graph analytics at scale. ### Audience Tiers

Every Echo has four visitor access levels. The creator controls knowledge depth, message limits, and pricing at each level through the Knowledge Vault's per-folder visibility settings.

**Guest.** No account required. Accessed via embedded widget on the creator's own website or through a shared link. The creator sets the message limit (default: 5 messages). Surface-level answers from the creator's Public-tagged knowledge only (podcasts, interviews, articles, social media). Purpose: let visitors experience the Echo and understand the format. Zero friction.

**Public.** Free Echoes account required. Access to the discovery page and interest-graph recommendations. The creator controls message limits (e.g., 20 messages per session). Answers draw from Public knowledge only. When a visitor reaches a question that requires deeper material, the Echo signals that deeper access is available through a paid deep session or Inner Circle membership.

**Inner Circle.** Invite-only groups created by the creator. Each group can have its own name (Members, VIP, Summer Cohort, Family, Close Friends), its own pricing, and its own knowledge access rules mapped to specific Knowledge Vault folders. Inner Circle members get access to deeper knowledge folders, relationship memory (the Echo remembers past conversations), proactive follow-up, and exclusive content.

**Private.** Visible only to the creator and their collaborators. Knowledge tagged as Private never surfaces to any visitor — not Guests, not Public users, not Inner Circle members. This is where the creator stores material they use to train the Echo's reasoning but do not want shared with anyone: personal journals, internal notes, sensitive frameworks, draft thinking. The Echo can reason from Private material (it shapes how the Echo thinks), but it never quotes or directly surfaces it in visitor-facing responses.

## Privacy, Security, and EU Data Sovereignty

Echoes asks people to trust it with their identity, knowledge, voice, and personal data. That trust has to be earned architecturally, not just promised in a privacy policy.

**All data stays in the EU.** Servers are located in EU data centers. No data is processed in or transferred to the US or any non-EU jurisdiction. This is not a policy decision that can be reversed. It is an infrastructure commitment built from day one.

**Encryption in transit and at rest.** All data in transit is encrypted (TLS 1.3). All data at rest is encrypted (AES-256). Conversation logs, corpus material, voice data, LoRA adapters, and personal information are encrypted on disk. Even if someone breaches storage, they get ciphertext.

**Data isolation per user.** Each creator's Echo is a separate LoRA adapter (40-100 MB file) with its own encrypted corpus. There is no shared model that absorbs user data. One user's information never leaks into another user's Echo. If a user deletes their account, their LoRA adapter and corpus are fully deleted. Nothing remains in a shared model because there is no shared model to contaminate.

**GDPR compliance is structural, not policy.** Article 17 (right to erasure) is trivial to comply with because the user's entire Echo is a deletable file, not a contribution to a monolithic model. Article 15 (right of access) is straightforward because every piece of data in the corpus is tagged, sourced, and exportable. Article 20 (data portability) is built in: the user can export their entire Echo at any time.

**EU AI Act compliance.** Echoes is designed with the EU AI Act in mind. Transparent provenance (every response cites its source), human oversight (the creator reviews and approves everything), and consent-first architecture align with the Act's requirements for high-risk AI systems.

**No competitor offers this.** Delphi is US-based (US infrastructure, US legal jurisdiction). Sentience is US-based. BuddyPro routes API calls through US providers. Coachvox is UK-based with no data residency guarantees. For European creators and users, and for any creator who cares about where their data lives, Echoes is the only EU-native option with structural GDPR compliance built into the architecture.
**Why this matters for creators.** A creator handing over their voice, their knowledge, their reasoning, and their identity needs to know that data is not being used to train someone else's model, is not sitting on a US server subject to US government data requests, and can be fully deleted if they leave. We guarantee all three by architecture, not by policy.

## Legal Framework (for investor Q&A)

Five architectural commitments cover the bulk of legal exposure:

1. **Pre-builds are strictly internal until claim.** No public surfacing of any real person's Echo without their explicit consent.
2. **Sealed identity + traceable provenance.** Every Echo response cites its source. Architectural answer to defamation and impersonation risk.
3. **EU data sovereignty + encryption.** All processing in EU infrastructure. All data encrypted in transit and at rest. Per-user data isolation. The creator's LoRA adapter is a 40-100 MB deletable file. Article 17 compliance is structural.
4. **Identity verification.** Private Echoes require phone only. Public Echoes require full KYC (government-issued ID + social media cross-link). Trust requirement scales with exposure surface.
5. **No training on user data for other purposes.** User data is used exclusively to build that user's Echo. It is never used to train a general model, never shared with other users, and never sold to third parties.

**Post-mortem handling.** GDPR explicitly applies only to living natural persons. After death, GDPR rights do not transfer; this is estate and succession law (varies by jurisdiction). We provide a clear product-level mechanism:

- Each user designates an **Executor** at any point in settings.
- On verified death, the Executor receives one-time access to either (a) **seal the Echo permanently** (no further edits, no further training, conversations continue at sealed state), or (b) **delete the Echo entirely** (full takedown, LoRA adapter deleted). Cannot modify the Echo's identity.
- Default if no Executor designated: Echo is sealed and made Private after a verification period.
